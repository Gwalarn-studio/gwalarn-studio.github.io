Remote Work in France: Your Rights and Costs (2026)

What the French Labour Code says, what your employer must or can cover, and what to check for tax and insurance, explained in plain English.

Updated November 21, 2026
In short
  • Remote work in France is governed by articles L.1222-9 to L.1222-11 of the Labour Code.
  • Remote workers have the same rights as on-site staff, and an accident at home during working hours is presumed to be a work accident.
  • Refusing remote work is not a ground for dismissal.
  • Employers can pay a remote work allowance, exempt from contributions up to an amount Urssaf sets each year.

Remote work is now part of daily life for millions of employees in France, but many people don't know their rights: who pays for the internet, what happens after an accident, can the employer impose or refuse remote work, do you still get meal vouchers? This guide sums up the rules for private-sector employees in France, in plain English, for French residents and expats alike.

The legal framework

Article L.1222-9 of the Labour Code defines remote work (télétravail) as any way of organising work in which work that could have been done on the employer's premises is done elsewhere, voluntarily, using information technology.

It is set up:

  • by a collective agreement (company or sector level),
  • or, failing that, by an employer charter, after consulting the staff council (CSE) if there is one,
  • or, with neither, by agreement between employee and employer, recorded by any means (writing is strongly advised).

The agreement or charter sets out, among other things, how to move to remote work and back, how working time is monitored, and the hours during which the employer can contact you.
