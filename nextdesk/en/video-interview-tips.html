Video Interview Tips: How to Ace an Interview on Camera

Everything that makes the difference on camera: tech prep, how to speak, the questions remote recruiters ask and what to do after the call.

Updated November 21, 2026
In short
  • Prepare a video interview the day before: link tested, updates installed, light in front of you, camera at eye level.
  • Look at the camera when you speak: that is what feels like eye contact to the recruiter.
  • For a remote role, the interview is already a test of how you work remotely.
  • Always have a plan B: the recruiter's phone number and a hotspot from your phone.

Video has become a standard hiring step, often from the first conversation, and sometimes all the way to the final round for remote roles. The questions are the same as in person. Everything else changes: the image, the sound, how you hold attention through a screen. This guide covers each point in the order you will need it.

Why video changes the game

In person, the recruiter picks up your handshake, your posture, the energy in the room. On video, they only see a rectangle: your face, your background and the quality of your sound. Every detail weighs more. A crackling mic tires the ear, a dark picture makes your face hard to read, eyes fixed on the screen look evasive.

For a remote role there is one more stake: your future employer is wondering how you will work on video every day. A candidate who is on time, well set up and calm through a dropped connection sends a strong signal: they are ready for remote work.
