How to Ask Your Employer to Work Remotely (With Template)

How to present your request so it gets accepted, a template to copy, what to do if the answer is no, and the key rules if you work in France.

Updated November 21, 2026
In short
  • Start by checking whether your company has a remote work policy: it often sets eligible roles, days and procedure.
  • A good request reassures: which tasks, how you stay reachable, how results are tracked, a trial period.
  • Ask for less at first: one day a week is easier to accept than three.
  • Make the request in writing and keep a record of the answer.

Already in a job and want to work from home one or more days a week? It is an increasingly common request, and many companies are open to it. How you present it makes all the difference: a vague request worries a manager, a precise one reassures them. This guide gives you the method, a template, and the key legal points if you work in France.

Check the rules first

Rules depend on your country and your company. Before asking, find out:

  • Is there a remote work policy? Ask HR or check the intranet. It usually sets eligible roles, the number of days, the procedure and sometimes an allowance.
  • What does your contract say? Some contracts fix the place of work.
  • What are the local rules? Some countries give employees a formal right to request flexible or remote work, with a procedure the employer must follow.

If you work in France: remote work is governed by article L.1222-9 of the Labour Code. It is set up by a collective agreement or an employer charter, or, failing that, by agreement between employer and employee by any means (ideally in writing). It is not an automatic right, but if your role is eligible under the agreement or charter, a refusal must be justified, and it must also be justified for a disabled worker or a family carer. Remote workers have the same rights as on-site staff, and an accident at the remote workplace during working hours is presumed to be a work accident.

Sources: French Labour Code, articles L.1222-9 to L.1222-11 (legifrance.gouv.fr); service-public.fr. Checked in November 2026.
