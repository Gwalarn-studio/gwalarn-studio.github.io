Remote Freelancing: How to Land Your First 3 Clients

A clear offer, a legal setup, a price you can live on, and a concrete method to land your first three clients.

Updated November 21, 2026
In short
  • Start with one precise offer for one type of client, not "I do a bit of everything".
  • Register your business through the official channel in your country; in France it is free.
  • Work out your rate from the income you want, contributions and unbilled time.
  • Your first clients are almost always in your network: former employers, colleagues, local businesses.

Salaried remote jobs are in high demand, and so fiercely contested. In many fields (writing, design, development, virtual assistance, translation, social media, bookkeeping) there is another route: working remotely as a freelancer for a few clients. The hardest part is not the paperwork, it is finding the first clients. This guide gives you a simple, step-by-step method.

Step 1: pick a simple offer

"Virtual assistant" or "content writer" doesn't say what you bring. A good offer fits in one sentence: I do [service] for [type of client] so that [result].

Vague offerClear offer
Virtual assistantI manage inboxes, calendars and quotes for building contractors.
Content writerI write product pages for home decor online shops.
Social media managerI run Instagram for local restaurants, 3 posts a week.
DeveloperI build and maintain websites for physiotherapy practices.

A precise offer is easier to sell, easier to price and easier to recommend.
