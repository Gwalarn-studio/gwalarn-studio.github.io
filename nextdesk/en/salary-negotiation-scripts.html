Salary Negotiation: Method and Ready-to-Use Scripts

Find the right number, know when to say it, and the exact words for each situation, for a new job or a raise.

Updated November 21, 2026
In short
  • A negotiation is won before the interview: arrive with three numbers (ideal, target, minimum).
  • Give a range whose bottom is your real target.
  • A polite, reasoned request rarely costs you an offer.
  • If the salary can't move, negotiate the rest: remote days, equipment, a 6-month review, training.

Many candidates accept the first number offered, for fear of looking demanding or losing the offer. That is often a costly mistake: your starting salary is the base for future raises, and a few hundred a year today repeats every year. Negotiating is not aggressive. It is a normal conversation recruiters have every day.

Why you should negotiate

Companies usually budget a range for each hire. The first offer rarely sits at the top of that range: it leaves room. If you ask for nothing, that room stays with the employer.

A reasonable request backed by facts almost never sinks a hire. At worst, you hear that the budget is fixed. The recruiter has spent time choosing you: they have no interest in starting over for a small amount.

In a current job, the logic is the same: a prepared raise request, made at the right time, is a professional move, not a favour.
