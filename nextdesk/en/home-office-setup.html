Home Office Setup on a Budget: A Remote Worker's Guide

The settings that prevent back pain, the purchases that really help in order of priority, and what your employer can cover.

Updated November 21, 2026
In short
  • Top of the screen at eye level, about an arm's length away: the setting that changes the most.
  • With a laptop, add a separate keyboard and mouse, then raise the screen.
  • Light comes from the side, never from behind the screen or behind you.
  • Before buying, ask what your employer provides or reimburses.

Eight hours a day hunched over a laptop on a kitchen chair: that is daily life for many remote workers, and a common cause of neck, shoulder and back pain. You don't need a corporate budget to work well at home. A few free adjustments and two or three well-chosen purchases are enough.

Choosing the spot

  • A dedicated space, even a corner of a room, that is not your bed or sofa. Your brain will link it with work, and you leave it in the evening.
  • Near a window, but not facing it or with your back to it: to the side.
  • Quiet, away from the TV and through-traffic.
  • Near the router, or where you can run a cable.
  • With a neutral background for video calls: a wall, a tidy shelf.
