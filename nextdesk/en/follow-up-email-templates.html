How to Follow Up on a Job Application (6 Email Templates)

The right timing, the right channel and the right words to follow up without being pushy, with six ready-to-send templates.

Updated November 21, 2026
In short
  • Follow up 7 to 10 working days after applying, and 3 to 5 working days after the reply date promised at an interview.
  • One follow-up per stage is enough. After that, move on.
  • A good follow-up is short, polite and adds something new.
  • Email is the safest channel; LinkedIn and phone are add-ons.

You sent a careful application… and nothing. No reply, not even a rejection. It is the most common situation in a job search, and it doesn't necessarily mean no: recruiters receive many applications, juggle several roles and processes slip. A well-made follow-up puts your file back at the top of the pile without making you look pushy.

Why following up makes a difference

A follow-up shows three things at once: genuine interest in the role, reliability, and the ability to move things forward, a quality many jobs look for. For a remote role, it is also a preview of how you communicate in writing.

Above all, it costs two minutes and loses you nothing. A recruiter who rejected you won't change their mind over a polite email; a swamped recruiter may reopen your file.
